# Asia Top 10 - Monday, 9 November 2026

**Today in one line:** New Zealand's tight election count and Taiwan's market-rescue fund headline a weekend of fragile Asian markets, ahead of ASEAN and APEC diplomacy.

## 1. New Zealand's coalition clings to a slim majority after knife-edge vote [Politics]

**What happened:** Preliminary counting on Sunday showed the National-ACT-NZ First coalition with 67 seats against 57 for Labour, the Greens and Te Pati Maori, a majority of just two on one tally. Prime Minister Luxon declared a mandate but conceded the result remained too close to call definitively; special votes are still to be counted, with official results due on 27 November.
**Why it matters:** Weeks of coalition bargaining could follow in a country whose government sets policy on China, trade and housing.
**Sources:** [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/amp/news/world/2026/11/08/who-will-govern-new-zealand-tight-election-result-opens-door-to-dealmaking/242459), [Cambodianess](https://cambodianess.com/article/new-zealand-poised-for-weeks-of-political-wrangling-after-knife-edge-poll), [China Daily HK](https://www.chinadailyhk.com/article/642382)

## 2. Taiwan activates market stabilisation fund after AI-led rout [Markets]

**What happened:** The management committee of Taiwan's National Financial Stabilization Fund authorised market intervention on Friday after the Taiex fell 1,873 points, or 4.51%, to close below 40,000, part of a 5.67% two-day drop on worries over heavy AI spending. Dow Jones reported the index jumped once the fund was activated. It was the tenth activation since 2000.
**Why it matters:** The intervention shows how exposed Taiwan's economy is to the AI-chip trade and sets up Monday's session as a test of confidence.
**Sources:** [Morningstar / Dow Jones](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202611081423/taiex-jumps-after-taiwan-activates-market-stabilization-fund), [Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202611060020)

## 3. Asian equities rebound but sentiment fragile after tech rout [Markets]

**What happened:** Asian equities mostly rebounded after the previous week's selloff, which saw the Nikkei fall about 6% and the Kospi about 9% on Friday after a $2.5 trillion Wall Street drop on US midterm worries, though Dow Jones described sentiment as fragile. A Kyunghyang report said the Kospi lost about 11% over two days and barely held 5,000.
**Why it matters:** Index swings of this size make Monday's open a key read on whether the AI trade has stabilised.
**Sources:** [Morningstar / Dow Jones](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202611081269/asian-equities-rebound-though-sentiment-remains-fragile), [Al Jazeera](https://aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/9/asian-stock-markets-climb-after-earlier-tech-rout), [Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202611061703007)

## 4. Delhi building collapse toll rises to seven [Disaster]

**What happened:** The death toll in the Chawri Bazar building collapse in Old Delhi rose to seven after another body was found on Sunday, after a rescue operation lasting more than 70 hours. Police have arrested the owner and three contractors.
**Why it matters:** The disaster raises questions about illegal construction in Delhi's dense historic districts.
**Sources:** [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/news-brief/death-toll-rises-to-seven-in-delhi-building-disaster-as-70-hour-rescue-operation-continues-in-hauz-qazi), [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/india/tragic-building-collapse-in-chawri-bazar/story)

## 5. Japan's Takaichi attends rally for return of abductees [Politics]

**What happened:** Prime Minister Takaichi attended a Tokyo citizens' rally on 8 November demanding the immediate return of all Japanese abducted by North Korea, according to her office's schedule; the week also included a nuclear emergency exercise and the launch of a new Disaster Management Agency. Her approval has slipped about 15 points in a year in one Korean poll.
**Why it matters:** The abduction issue remains the main obstacle to any Japan-North Korea thaw and is a signature priority for Takaichi.
**Sources:** [Japan PM Office](https://japan.kantei.go.jp/105/actions/index.html), [Herald Corp](https://mbiz.heraldcorp.com/article/10922912)

## 6. China to host a Pakistani astronaut at Tiangong; energy-investment report [Tech] (unconfirmed)

**What happened:** A global news digest said China is preparing to host a Pakistani astronaut at its Tiangong space station; earlier official statements said a Pakistani payload specialist would join Shenzhou-24 in late 2026, the first foreign astronaut to visit. A separate report projected China will lead world energy investment in 2026 at about US$940 billion.
**Why it matters:** The flight would showcase China's space partnerships, while energy spending underlines Beijing's role in the Iran-war energy shock.
**Sources:** [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/pakistan/pakistans-first-astronaut-to-join-chinas-space-mission-1.500327385), [The Standard Night Recap](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/347896/Night-Recap-November-8-2026)

## 7. Southeast Asia headlines: Thailand courts OECD entry; Indonesia tightens borders; Manila finalises summit [Economy] (unconfirmed)

**What happened:** A Bernama roundup for 8 November reported that Thai Prime Minister Anutin is pushing faster OECD accession and a "Thailand FastPass" for investors, that Indonesia is strengthening border security through an Interpol alert system, and that Finance Secretary Recto is overseeing final preparations for the ASEAN Summit.
**Why it matters:** Governments are positioning for the Manila summit and for investment inflows amid energy and market stress.

## 8. Philippines' US-backed "Pax Silica" chip hub lacks tenants, power and water [Economy]

**What happened:** The 1,620-hectare site at New Clark City in Tarlac has no confirmed tenants, and the BCDA chief says it could create nearly 200,000 jobs over a decade. SCMP reported the hub lacks tenants, power and water; lawmakers worry about grid strain and Aeta communities face relocation.
**Why it matters:** The project is central to Washington's plan to shift chip supply chains to allies, and its delays complicate Manila's pitch at the ASEAN summit.
**Sources:** [Gulf News (AFP)](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/philippines/philippine-tribe-in-limbo-over-us-led-tech-hub-1.500681039), [Inquirer Business](https://business.inquirer.net/604771/pax-silica-site-development-eyed-in-3-to-5-years)

## 9. Beijing confirms talks on a Xi-Trump-Putin meeting at APEC [Diplomacy]

**What happened:** China confirmed that discussions are under way on a possible three-way Xi-Trump-Putin meeting on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen on 18-19 November, the Korea Times reported. Foreign-ministry listings also showed Xi will attend the leaders' meeting.
**Why it matters:** A meeting of the three nuclear powers would be historic, but the format and agenda remain unsettled.
**Sources:** [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20261107/china-confirms-discussions-underway-on-historic-xi-trump-putin-meeting), [China Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/eng/)

## 10. Iran war keeps Asia's energy supply under strain [Economy / Security]

**What happened:** Months into the Iran war, flows through the Strait of Hormuz remain far below pre-war levels. Asia absorbed 87% of the crude and 86% of the LNG that transited the strait in 2025, and the Philippines declared a national energy emergency. Reports in September flagged a marine-fuel shortage as well.
**Why it matters:** Southeast Asia's oil shortages are a leading item on the ASEAN summit agenda and fuel inflation across the region.
**Sources:** [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/28/iran-war-hormuz-blocked-asia-energy-crisis-oil-gas/), [Al Jazeera](https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/7/iran-and-ukraine-wars-why-ship-fuel-is-running-short-and-why-it-matters)
