# Hong Kong Top 10 - Friday, 6 November 2026

**Today in one line:** Joshua Wong was sentenced to 10 years under the national security law, a worker died at a Sha Tin roadworks site, and the Hang Seng jumped 3.67% after the US midterms.

## 1. Joshua Wong sentenced to 10 years for conspiring with foreign forces [Courts]

**What happened:** The High Court sentenced Joshua Wong to 10 years on Thursday for conspiracy to collude with foreign forces under the national security law. He had pleaded guilty on 2 September. Prosecutors said he worked with exiled activist Nathan Law and others in 2020 to press foreign governments for sanctions. The judge said the sanctions advocated could be highly detrimental to Hong Kong and the mainland. Human Rights Watch and Amnesty criticised the sentence; Beijing's foreign ministry said Hong Kong upholds the rule of law. He was already serving a separate sentence, and one report puts his release date at late 2036.
**Why it matters:** It is the latest major national security sentence and a diplomatic flashpoint.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/347574/Joshua-Wong-sentenced-to-10-years-over-foreign-collusion-charge), [Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/11/05/hong-kong-joshua-wong-gets-nearly-10-more-years-in-prison), [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/11/hong-kong-pro-democracy-activist-sentenced-to-ten-years-imprisonment-over-charges-of-conspiring-with-foreign-forces/)

## 2. Hang Seng surges 3.67% to 24,796.98 [Markets]

**What happened:** The Hang Seng Index rose 877.48 points, or 3.67%, to 24,796.98 on Thursday on turnover of HK$356.67 billion. The China Enterprises Index rose 3.87% to 8,369.08 and the Tech Index 5.55% to 4,154.29. Alibaba rose 8.5% and Tencent 7.2%. The Standard attributed the rally to improved sentiment after the US midterm elections, with tech and gold stocks leading.
**Why it matters:** It lifted the index back toward 25,000 and reversed the week's earlier softness.
**Sources:** [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261105/30dca13060c845e8aeb2926ad0009301/c.html), [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/347630/Hang-Seng-Index-surges-nearly-900-points-at-close)

## 3. Government condemns US Consulate remarks on Joshua Wong case [Politics]

**What happened:** The HKSAR government strongly condemned remarks by a US Consulate General spokesman on the Wong sentence, urging the spokesman to stop "non-constructive and irresponsible" remarks and "fabricating facts".
**Why it matters:** It opens a diplomatic exchange that widened with a ten-nation statement the next day.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/347683/HKSAR-Govt-strongly-condemns-US-Consulate-spokesmans-remarks-on-Joshua-Wong-case)

## 4. Woman worker killed at Sha Tin roadworks site [Society]

**What happened:** A 61-year-old woman worker was struck by a truck at a Sha Tin Road site, part of the Sha Tin Trunk Road T4 project, at about 11am on Thursday. The Standard calls it a grab truck while police-based reports describe a medium goods vehicle. The Civil Engineering and Development Department ordered its contractor to investigate.
**Why it matters:** It renews concern about safety on public works sites.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/347650/Woman-worker-killed-after-being-struck-by-truck-at-Sha-Tin-roadworks-site), [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/347695/Sha-Tin-roadworks-death-contractor-ordered-to-investigate-as-Labour-Department-halts-all-works-on-carriageway)

## 5. John Lee at China International Import Expo and Hongqiao forum [Politics]

**What happened:** Chief Executive John Lee attended the opening of the ninth CIIE in Shanghai on Thursday, accompanying Vice-Premier Ding Xuexiang, and led a record delegation of over 400 Hong Kong enterprises. He spoke at the Hongqiao International Economic Forum, saying Hong Kong needs forward-looking plans amid changing industrial chains and will strengthen its role as an offshore renminbi hub.
**Why it matters:** It highlights the government's economic-integration drive during a Beijing and Shanghai trip.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/347659/John-Lee-accompanies-Ding-Xuexiang-at-CIIE-in-Shanghai), [SCMP](https://scmp.com/news/hong-kong/hong-kong-economy/article/3373377/hong-kong-needs-forward-looking-plans-amid-changes-industrial-chains-john-lee)

## 6. Grandmother jailed 20 days for slapping girl at Kwun Tong mall [Courts]

**What happened:** Leung Lai-ha, 68, was sentenced to 20 days in jail after pleading guilty to assaulting a five-year-old girl at a play area in APM in Kwun Tong on 26 August. Police had said she was first struck in the earlier fight; two other women were bound over.
**Why it matters:** The viral case has been followed since August.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/347629/Elderly-woman-jailed-for-slapping-5-year-old-in-APM-fight), [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/law-and-crime/article/3373430/hong-kong-grandmother-gets-20-days-slapping-five-year-old-girl-play-area)

## 7. National security police arrest man over importing seditious books [Courts]

**What happened:** The National Security Department arrested a man over importing seditious publications for local bookstores. Later reports say he is a 34-year-old company director suspected of importing the books from Taiwan, and that the prisoner support group Waiting Bird says its ordered books went missing in a shipment.
**Why it matters:** It extends enforcement to book imports and civil-society groups.

## 9. Abby Choi trial: victim could have been hit seven times, jury told [Courts]

**What happened:** The murder trial heard that Abby Choi could have been struck on the head seven times with a hammer (SCMP headline). On Wednesday the court heard that DNA on the hammer handle matches her former husband Alex Kwong, while the hammer head sample was too small to analyse; no trace of Choi's DNA was detected on the hammer. All three defendants deny murder.
**Why it matters:** It is the city's most closely followed murder trial, expected to run about eight weeks.
**Sources:** [SCMP](https://scmp.com/news/hong-kong/law-and-crime/article/3373421/abby-choi-could-have-been-hit-head-seven-times-hammer-murder-trial-told), [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/347484/Ex-husbands-DNA-found-on-hammer-handle-court-hears-in-Abby-Choi-trial)
