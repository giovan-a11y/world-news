# Hong Kong Top 10 - Friday, 27 November 2026

**Today in one line:** Hong Kong stocks are bouncing after a deep sell-off, LegCo is busy with development and shipping bills, and the cold-snap season has arrived.

## 1. Hang Seng rebounds after a long losing streak [Markets]

**What happened:** One report put the Hang Seng's 25 November close at 21,966, below 22,000 for the first time in this sell-off. Another says it snapped a seven-session losing streak on 26 November, with a briefing citing a close around 22,227 (+1.2%) and a 13.3% fall for 2026. These figures are unreconciled (unconfirmed).
**Why it matters:** The index is well below the 24,000-25,000 levels seen earlier in November, reflecting the global AI-chip rout and tariff worries.
**Sources:** [BBN Times](https://www.bbntimes.com/companies/hong-kong-stock-exchange-hang-seng-falls-to-21-9666-01-amid-us-tariff-and-geopolitical-tensions), [The Library](https://thelibraryestate.com/hong-kong-markets-mid-morning-update-hang-seng-snaps-seven-session-losing-streak-nov-26-2026/), [Peter Lewis briefing](https://peterlewismoneytalk.substack.com/p/peters-asian-business-and-finance-d6c)

## 2. Northern Metropolis Development Bill passes [Government]

**What happened:** LegCo passed the Northern Metropolis Development Bill at third reading with government amendments, aiming to streamline development procedures across six policy areas.
**Why it matters:** It is the legal backbone for the government's flagship northern New Territories development.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/348346/Night-Recap-November-12-2026)

## 3. Teacher certification bill [Society]

**What happened:** The government planned to gazette an Education (Amendment) Bill 2026 requiring practising certificates for all teachers and barring anyone convicted of national security offences from teaching.
**Why it matters:** It tightens oversight of the teaching profession.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/348346/Night-Recap-November-12-2026)

## 4. LegCo week: shipping tax concessions and US report rebuttal [Government]

**What happened:** LegCo's schedule for 23-27 November includes a bill on shipping-related tax concessions and a statement rejecting a US-China Economic and Security Review Commission report.
**Why it matters:** Shipping incentives support Hong Kong's bid to stay a maritime hub as Gulf disruption hits trade routes.
**Sources:** [LegCo](https://www.legco.gov.hk/en/)

## 5. Cold weather and monsoon swings [Weather]

**What happened:** A northeast monsoon was forecast from 24 November with cooler, showery weather midweek, improving later, with highs near 26C on Saturday 28 November and 19-24C on Sunday. Earlier cold fronts dropped Tai Mo Shan to 6.9C on 10 November.
**Why it matters:** Sharp temperature swings affect health, transport and heating demand. Check the Observatory for the latest forecast.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/349211/Hong-Kong-temperatures-set-to-rebound-briefly-before-northeast-monsoon-brings-midweek-chill), [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/health-environment/article/3374766/bundle-hong-kong-temperatures-plunge-15-degrees-cold-front-hits-friday)

## 6. Hong Kong shipping and trade exposed to the Hormuz crisis [Economy]

**What happened:** Hormuz traffic is down about 40 percent, disrupting Gulf-Asia energy and cargo flows that run through Hong Kong's ports and shippers (impact on local firms not confirmed).
**Why it matters:** Higher freight and fuel costs can feed into local inflation and trade volumes.
**Sources:** [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/24/hormuz-oil-traffic-is-down-40-percent-are-iran-s-tanker-attacks-working)

## 7. Jimmy Lai case aftermath continues to shape rhetoric [Courts]

**What happened:** The government continues to defend the Court of First Instance's December 2025 conviction and subsequent sentencing against foreign criticism (background; no new ruling found today).
**Why it matters:** The case remains central to Hong Kong's international image.
**Sources:** [news.gov.hk](https://www.news.gov.hk/eng/2026/02/20260209/20260209_220905_404.html), [news.gov.hk](https://www.news.gov.hk/eng/2025/12/20251215/20251215_130232_172.html)
