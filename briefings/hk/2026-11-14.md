# Hong Kong Top 10 - Saturday, 14 November 2026

**Today in one line:** LegCo passed a Northern Metropolis bill and the Hang Seng fell 1.55% on Friday, while police upgraded a Central assault to murder.

## 1. Hang Seng falls 1.55% to 23,637.60 on Friday [Markets]

**What happened:** The Hang Seng Index closed at 23,637.60 on 13 November, down 370.95 points, after a 1.26% fall on Thursday. The tech index fell about 3% on Nvidia-related worries.
**Why it matters:** The tech-heavy market is exposed to the AI trade, with Nvidia reporting on 17 November.
**Sources:** [BBN Times](https://www.bbntimes.com/financial/hong-kong-stock-exchange-today-hang-seng-index-drops-1-55-to-23-637-60-as-tech-index-sinks-3-on-nvidia-fallout)

## 2. LegCo passes bill to streamline Northern Metropolis development [Government]

**What happened:** Lawmakers passed a bill to speed up Northern Metropolis development (details of the bill not confirmed in my sources).
**Why it matters:** It is a flagship land-supply and growth plan for the city.
**Sources:** [Hong Kong Free Press](https://hongkongfp.com/hong-kong-news/)

## 3. Central wounding case reclassified as murder [Society]

**What happened:** Police reclassified a wounding case in Central as murder after a 41-year-old Filipino man, assaulted on 7 November, died in hospital.
**Why it matters:** It is a violent-crime case in a busy district; the investigation continues.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/348388/Morning-Recap-November-13-2026)

## 4. Five defendants in San Po Kong subversion case intend to plead guilty [Courts]

**What happened:** The court heard all five defendants intend to plead guilty. They were among 10 people arrested in December last year on suspicion of illegal weapons training in an industrial building in San Po Kong.
**Why it matters:** The case moves towards sentencing under national-security law.
**Sources:** [Hong Kong Free Press](https://hongkongfp.com/hong-kong-news/)

## 5. Queen Mary Hospital lift probe finds "deficiencies" [Health]

**What happened:** A probe into the Queen Mary Hospital lift scandal found "deficiencies" and possible illegal acts (headline only; details unconfirmed).
**Why it matters:** It concerns safety and accountability at a major public hospital.
**Sources:** [Hong Kong Free Press](https://hongkongfp.com/hong-kong-news/)

## 6. Police arrest 12 in illegal ride-hailing crackdown [Transport]

**What happened:** Police arrested 12 men and seized 12 private cars in a citywide crackdown on illegal ride-hailing between 27 October and Friday.
**Why it matters:** It signals continued enforcement against unlicensed passenger services.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/348388/Morning-Recap-November-13-2026)

## 7. Court rejects Ronson Chan's bid to take appeal to top court [Courts]

**What happened:** The court rejected the former press union head's bid to take his police-obstruction appeal to the Court of Final Appeal (headline only; unconfirmed).
**Why it matters:** It ends a case closely watched by journalists.
**Sources:** [Hong Kong Free Press](https://hongkongfp.com/hong-kong-news/)

## 8. Northern Metropolis logistics cluster draws 16 expressions of interest [Economy]

**What happened:** Authorities said on Friday that 16 submissions came from developers, logistics operators, industry associations and professional services providers.
**Why it matters:** It tests market appetite for the Northern Metropolis plan.
**Sources:** [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong)

## 9. Education bill to tighten teacher registration and national security rules [Government]

**What happened:** The government planned to gazette the Education (Amendment) Bill 2026, requiring teaching certificates and barring people convicted of national security offences from teaching (reported 12 November).
**Why it matters:** It changes who can work in schools.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/348346/Night-Recap-November-12-2026)

## 10. Alphabet's first Hong Kong and Singapore dollar bonds draw about US$5 billion in orders [Economy]

**What happened:** Alphabet's first Hong Kong and Singapore dollar bond sales drew about US$5 billion in early orders.
**Why it matters:** It signals depth in the city's debt market.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/348346/Night-Recap-November-12-2026)
