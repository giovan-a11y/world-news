# Hong Kong Top 10 - Thursday, 26 November 2026

**Today in one line:** The Hang Seng sank below 22,000 on a global chip sell-off as Hong Kong marks the first anniversary of the Tai Po Wang Fuk Court fire.

*Editor's note: web access was limited today. Only a handful of Hong Kong stories could be sourced responsibly; items are marked where single-sourced.*

## 1. Hang Seng closes below 22,000 on chip sell-off [Markets]

**What happened:** The Hang Seng Index closed at 21,966.01 on 25 November, down 253 points or 1.14%, a one-year low per one report, on turnover of HK$229.9 billion. The Hang Seng Tech Index fell 2.56% to 3,378. Hua Hong Semiconductor dropped 17.3%, CATL 12.2% and Lenovo 11.1%, while JD.com rose 3.4%.
**Why it matters:** Hong Kong's heavy tech and chip weighting makes it vulnerable to the global AI sell-off.
**Sources:** [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/349608/Hang-Seng-Index-closes-below-22000-marks), [MarketScreener](https://marketscreener.com/news/hong-kong-stocks-fall-on-wednesday-as-global-tech-sell-off-deepens-alibaba-slips-1-ce7851dbd18ef624)

## 2. First anniversary of the Tai Po fire [Society]

**What happened:** 26 November marks one year since the Wang Fuk Court fire that killed 168 people. Survivors are mourning losses and questioning accountability.
**Why it matters:** The anniversary tests public trust in the government's reform and accountability promises.
**Sources:** [Straits Times via NST](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/world/world/2026/11/1585478/one-year-after-hong-kong-fire-survivors-mourn-losses-and-question&gsessionid=N_nwssrK30-Uy-OFjBGMZPvwtg2e6u9_Dn6z8f-fNuFZzFWo-g9TZA), [The News](https://www.thenews.com.pk/latest/1425009-hong-kong-fire-survivors-mourn-one-year-later)

## 3. Security chief defends police checks on memorial events [Politics]

**What happened:** Security Secretary Chris Tang defended police inquiries around anniversary events, citing risk assessments and "sedition" risks, while saying lawful memorials are allowed. Human Rights Watch said authorities threatened and arrested people criticising the response.
**Why it matters:** It raises questions over how commemoration and criticism are policed.
**Sources:** [HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/11/25/tai-po-fire-hong-kong-security-chief-defends-police-enquiries-surrounding-memorial-events-citing-sedition-risks/), [HRW](https://hrw.org/news/2026/11/25/hong-kong-fire-anniversary-underscores-growing-repression)

## 4. Beijing praises the government's fire response [Politics]

**What happened:** The Hong Kong and Macao Affairs Office said the response reflected responsibility and "strong resolve", pointing to relief efforts and planned systemic reforms.
**Why it matters:** It signals central-government backing as the SAR faces criticism.
**Sources:** [HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/11/25/hong-kong-govt-response-to-tai-po-fire-reflects-responsibility-and-strong-resolve-beijing-says/)

## 5. Most killed helpers' families still await compensation [Society]

**What happened:** Families of nine of the ten domestic helpers killed were still awaiting compensation a year on; the Indonesian consulate said two of nine claims had been submitted.
**Why it matters:** It highlights gaps in support for migrant workers.
**Sources:** [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/society/article/3375666/families-9-out-10-helpers-killed-tai-po-blaze-still-awaiting-compensation)

## 6. Reform review and new grants (partly unconfirmed) [Government]

**What happened:** Eric Chan leads the review of the independent committee's recommendations, and a reform panel held its first meeting. A headline also said the government ruled out an official memorial and announced HK$630 million in victim grants (single source; unconfirmed).
**Why it matters:** These steps will show how far the promised overhaul of building and fire safety goes.
**Sources:** [SCMP topic page](https://www.scmp.com/topics/hong-kongs-tai-po-fire-tragedy), [Tai Po fire support site](https://www.taipofire.gov.hk/eng/press.html)

## 7. Interpol General Assembly arrives next week [Security]

**What happened:** The 94th Interpol General Assembly runs 30 November to 3 December in Hong Kong, with 2,000+ delegates, and will consider a "silver notice" for tracing criminal assets. A report of a citywide drone ban and road curbs is unconfirmed.
**Why it matters:** It is a major international event for the city, with security measures likely to affect traffic.
**Sources:** [Interpol](https://www.interpol.int/en/News-and-Events/Events/2026/94th-INTERPOL-General-Assembly), [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/law-and-crime/article/3372074/interpols-hong-kong-meeting-consider-silver-notice-targeting-criminal-assets)
